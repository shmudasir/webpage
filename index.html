Research Interests

My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, probabilistic machine learning, statistical signal processing, and wireless communications. I work on Bayesian learning and variational inference for high-mobility communications, integrated sensing and communication, and near-field wireless systems, with a growing focus on AI-native architectures for beyond-6G networks, quantum communication, and foundation models for communication systems.

Sparse Bayesian Learning for OTFS Channel Estimation

Fractional delay and Doppler spread each target's energy across neighboring delay–Doppler bins, producing a channel that is block-sparse and correlated rather than sparse in the classical sense. I design structured priors and EM-based algorithms that learn this block structure directly, rather than assuming it is known in advance.

Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC-OTFS)

In ISAC-OTFS systems, the same waveform used for communication also senses the environment. I work on off-grid delay-Doppler channel estimators that turn this block-sparse structure into accurate range and velocity estimates for multiple targets.

RIS/IRS-Assisted OTFS Systems

Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS/IRS) introduce a cascaded BS–RIS–UE channel. I show that this cascade remains block-sparse in the delay-Doppler domain, and design estimators and phase-optimization schemes that exploit it for high-mobility RIS-OTFS networks.

Cell-Free Massive MIMO with IRS

In cell-free mMIMO networks, an IRS can restore connectivity when a direct AP–UE link is blocked. I develop learning-based channel estimators for these IRS-aided cell-free OTFS systems and analyze the resulting spectral efficiency gains.

Massive MIMO and Multi-User Channel Sparsity

Stacking the delay-Doppler-angle channels of multiple users reveals a burst-block sparsity pattern shared across users. I design joint, variational estimators that exploit this shared structure for massive MIMO-OTFS uplink channel estimation.

High-Mobility Multi-Frame Channel Tracking

The same scatterers, and therefore the same sparse support, persist across several consecutive OTFS frames. I develop multi-frame estimators that track this shared support jointly instead of re-estimating each frame independently, improving accuracy under fractional Doppler.
